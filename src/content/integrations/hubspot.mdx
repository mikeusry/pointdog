## The gap between a HubSpot lead and a Google conversion

Most HubSpot setups tell Google Ads one thing: *a form was submitted.* HubSpot knows much
more. It knows which of those contacts became a sales-qualified lead, which ones opened a
deal, and which deals closed and for how much.

Google, Meta, and Microsoft don't know any of that unless you send it. So they optimize for
form fills, and your sales team keeps getting leads that look nothing like your customers.

## What we build

1. **Click ID capture on every form.** gclid, gbraid/wbraid, fbc, and msclkid saved as
   HubSpot contact properties, plus hashed email and phone as a fallback. See
   [click IDs explained](/guides/click-ids-gclid-fbclid-msclkid).
2. **Deal-stage conversions.** When a deal hits a stage you care about (meeting held,
   proposal sent, closed-won), a sync sends that conversion with the **actual deal amount**,
   or a predicted value for early stages.
3. **Platform sends.** Google through the
   [Data Manager API](/guides/google-ads-data-manager-api), Meta through the
   [Conversions API](/guides/meta-conversions-api), and Microsoft offline conversions.
4. **Corrections.** Deals that fall apart or get refunded are sent back as adjustments.

## Data flowing the other way

HubSpot is often where sales lives, but not where the customer history lives. For our own
companies we push lifetime value, purchase history, and customer segments from our data
warehouse into HubSpot every day, so a rep opening a contact sees what that customer
actually buys before picking up the phone.

## When HubSpot's native sync is enough

If you have a short sales cycle, one product, and deals that are all roughly the same size,
HubSpot's built-in ad conversion events may be all you need. Turn them on first. We come in
when deal values vary, when there's revenue outside HubSpot, or when you need Microsoft Ads
and audiences built from deal data.
